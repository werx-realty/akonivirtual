What your VA does — and doesn't.

Our VAs are liaisons. You stay in control of every contract and every dollar.

Your VA handles

  • Lead follow-up, nurture calls and texts
  • Setting showings, including through MLS and ShowingTime
  • Following the deal and keeping escrow on track
  • Updating clients and keeping you informed daily
  • Calendar, tasks, documents and reports

Always stays with you

  • Writing, negotiating or signing contracts
  • Handling earnest money, wires or any funds
  • Giving legal or financial advice to clients
  • Sharing your data with any other agent

The forms we handle every day.

The forms your VA handles on every file, explained simply. Tap any one to learn more.

Proof of Funds

Proves your buyer has the money to close, using a recent account statement or a note from their bank.

View document

Extension of Time Addendum

Buys more time when a deadline is coming too fast, like a contingency period or the closing date, once both sides agree.

View document

Natural Hazard Disclosure

Lets buyers know if the property is in a flood, wildfire, earthquake or other state-mapped risk area.

View document

Transfer Disclosure Statement

The seller's list of everything they know about the home, from past repairs to known problems and neighborhood issues.

View document

California Form 593

California's real estate withholding form. Escrow uses it on almost every sale to figure out how much state tax to hold back.

View document

Contingency Removal

The form a buyer signs to drop the inspection, appraisal or loan contingency and move the deal closer to closing.

View document

FIRPTA Affidavit

The seller says if they count as foreign for U.S. tax rules, which decides if some of their proceeds get held back.

View document

Buyer Representation Agreement

Your written deal with a buyer client: how you're paid, what you'll do and how long it lasts.

View document

Addendum

A signed change or add-on to the deal after the contract is already in place.

Tips & insights

What agents should know right now.

Website tips

Google's spam updates and agent websites

Templated neighborhood pages that only swap the city name are what Google's scaled-content rules target. Write fewer local pages, and fill each one with things only someone who works that area would know.

Software

Running two CRMs without losing referrals

Many agents use a brokerage platform plus their own CRM. When the two never sync, referrals fall through the gap. Clean your contacts first, sync a small test group, then connect the rest.

Marketing

The quiet wealthy second-home buyer

Local business owners like car dealers, dentists and restaurant groups hold huge wealth and buy quietly, often with cash. They want numbers, privacy and a clean file, not glossy copy.

Transaction support

Breaking the 9-deal ceiling

On their own, most agents top out around nine deals a year, while teams do several times that. It comes down to time, not skill. Hand off deadlines, disclosures, document chasing and escrow follow-up to free up selling hours.
