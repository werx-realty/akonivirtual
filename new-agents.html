Year one, honestly

Your first year, you're learning three jobs at the same time. Most new agents feel stretched thin because they are.

  • Finding clients. Lead generation, follow-up and turning your sphere into business
  • Contracts and disclosures. Your state's forms, deadlines and rules
  • Running the deal. Keeping every date, document and person on track from accepted offer to keys

A single transaction can take dozens of hours of admin work. That's time a new agent badly needs for finding the next client.

California disclosures that trip up new agents

Every state is different, but California shows how much paperwork a new agent is expected to know on day one.

Transfer Disclosure StatementThe seller lists what they know about the home, and the listing agent adds what they saw on a walk-through. Learn more
Natural Hazard DisclosureFlags flood, wildfire, earthquake and other mapped risks for the property. Learn more
Agency DisclosureSpells out who you work for in the deal: one side or both.
Seller Property QuestionnaireNot required by law, but widely used to get more detail from the seller.

California rules in effect for 2026

Buyer agreements up front. You need a signed written agreement with your buyer, covering pay and scope, before you work with them. See the agreement

Flip disclosures. A seller who resells soon after buying has to list the work they did on the home, like added space or structural fixes.

Listing length limits. Exclusive listing agreements on single-family homes have a maximum length, with shorter limits on renewals.

A 90-day starter plan

A simple plan to go from new license to a real pipeline.

Weeks 1–2

Get set up

  • Get your cards printed and your lockbox app working
  • Load everyone you know into a CRM, your first pipeline
  • Write your agent bio and update your social profiles
  • Find a mentor in your office you can call with questions
Weeks 3–4

Know your area

  • Check new listings, solds and price changes every day
  • Offer free home value reports to people you know
  • Ask to host open houses for busy agents
  • Tag along with a top producer on showings and listing appointments
Months 2–3

Fill your pipeline

  • Make 10 contacts every morning before anything else
  • Write down what you'll spend and what you want to earn in 90 days
  • Join a local group, chamber or community board
  • Block time each week for prospecting, learning and admin

Rookie mistakes to skip

1

Underestimating the paperwork

Deadlines and disclosures stack up fast. Missing one can cost you a deal and your reputation.

2

Doing everything alone

Mentors and support are there for a reason. Doing it all yourself is how new agents burn out.

3

Stopping prospecting mid-deal

Your first escrow eats your week, then you close and have nothing next. Keep reaching out every day.

4

Not tracking your time

If you don't know where your hours go, you can't tell what to hand off.
